THE HOUSE YOUR CAT KNOWS

Small ways to notice and save the life you share. This digital archive lets you attach your own real photos, videos and sounds to the same activities used by the print book.

The ZIP is your archive. This page is where you work on it — Save your archive when you are done, and Open an archive ZIP to carry on, on this device or any other.

On a phone or tablet? Open this archive in your browser instead: quiet-days-books.github.io/the-house-your-cat-knows. A file opened from your phone’s Files app can show this page but cannot save anything.

On iPhone or iPad: tap Share → Add to Home Screen and open the archive from there. Safari keeps a home-screen archive; in an ordinary tab it may clear what you saved after a week without a visit. Save a ZIP either way.

Each cat has its own archive here. Switching cats above changes which notes and files you are looking at; nothing is mixed and nothing is lost. Files stay on this device, in this browser. Use Save your archive to keep a copy you can open anywhere, and keep your original photos, videos and sounds too. Download a backup on the first day, not on the day you need it — clearing this browser’s data removes the archive.

A Map of Their Routes

Notice

Your cat probably has a map of the house that no one else has drawn. It may run under chairs, around one table leg, over the same rug and through a doorway they pause in every time.

Try This

Watch one normal route without directing it. Later, make a simple sketch of the path. Mark rooms, thresholds, surfaces, pauses and the places they avoid.

If Needed

For a low-mobility cat, map the route from bed to bowl, litter box, window or favorite resting place.

Keep Safe

Do not close doors, move furniture or call your cat through a route for the page.

Save It

Save the sketch or a photo of it in Places.

If Today Is Quiet

If there is no route today, write down the place where they stayed. Stillness is also geography.

One Day in This House

Notice

Choose one day only because you are already living it. Not a perfect day. Not a last day. Not a day arranged around your cat. Just a day in this house while your cat is here.

Try This

Across the day, save a few details that happen on their own: one place, one sound, one route, one distance, one object, one sentence. Use any mix that fits.

If Needed

If the day is too busy, collect one detail. If your cat spends the day resting, record the resting place and the room around it.

Keep Safe

Do not use a timer, wake your cat, stage a scene or require a certain number of files.

Save It

Save the day's notes and files in One Day.

If Today Is Quiet

If the day produces almost nothing, write one line: This was the day we had.
